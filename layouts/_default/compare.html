Compared to the status quo

Traceability tools show you the graph. Provenance enforces it.

Greenlight Guru, Jama Connect, Polarion, Codebeamer, and MasterControl all do e-signatures, audit logging, and traceability visibility well — that's established, table-stakes territory, and Provenance implements it too. The gap is what happens between the visibility those tools stop at and the enforcement a regulated team actually needs.

Compared to open-source tools

Docs-as-code tools already keep requirements in git.

Doorstop, StrictDoc, and Sphinx-Needs share Provenance's starting point: plain-text records under version control, reviewed like code. They are built for general systems and safety engineering, not for design controls under 21 CFR Part 11, and none of them records who approved a change. Provenance is pre-release, so capabilities it does not have yet are marked planned.

Nothing here reinvents e-signatures

Signer identity, timestamps, reauthentication, and tamper-evidence are established, expected capabilities, and Provenance's signature design draws directly on how the mature implementations already do it. The real gaps are structural: an open, user-accessible storage format; a hosting model that doesn't default to a vendor's cloud; enforcement that actually blocks a bad change instead of just flagging it; and an offline artifact a reviewer can open without installing anything.
